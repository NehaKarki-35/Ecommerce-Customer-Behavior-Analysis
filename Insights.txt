Insights:

Customer churn is not caused by a single factor — it is a gradual behavioral process influenced by satisfaction, engagement, pricing sensitivity, support experience, loyalty effectiveness, and purchasing behavior.

1. Customers with high engagement and satisfaction are more loyal, active, and likely to continue purchasing regularly.

2. Low engagement, long inactivity, and declining satisfaction strongly indicate high churn risk among customers.

3. Many customers frequently browse products but place few orders, showing conversion barriers such as pricing or checkout issues.

4. High support ticket volumes often reduce customer satisfaction and increase churn probability.

5. Effective customer support can still retain dissatisfied customers, creating the Support Paradox Effect.

6. Customers heavily dependent on discounts are usually less loyal and more likely to churn when promotions stop.

7. Excessive discount dependency creates a Discount Addiction Loop where customers buy only during offers.

8. Loyalty programs improve retention, but loyalty alone cannot prevent churn without good customer experience.

9. Highly price-sensitive customers are more unstable and can easily switch to competitors when engagement decreases.

10. Customer churn is generally a slow process caused by declining engagement, reduced purchases, and growing dissatisfaction over time.

11. High return rates, poor support experience, and low satisfaction scores are major contributors to customer churn.

12. Overall, customer retention depends on strong engagement, satisfaction, personalized experience, quality support, and value-driven relationships rather than discounts alone.
